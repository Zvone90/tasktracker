Tattoo Aftercare: A Complete Guide to Keeping Your New Tattoo Healthy

August 5, 2026

A new tattoo is more than just artwork — it is an open wound that needs time and proper care to heal. Good aftercare helps protect the tattoo from infection, reduces unnecessary irritation, and gives the skin the best chance of healing while keeping the artwork looking sharp.

Your tattoo artist may recommend a specific aftercare routine depending on the technique, ink, placement, and covering they used. Always follow your artist’s instructions if they differ from general advice.

The First Few Hours

Immediately after your tattoo is finished, your artist will usually cover it with either traditional plastic wrap or a specialized adhesive tattoo film.

The covering protects the fresh tattoo from dirt, bacteria, friction, and contact with clothing.

How long you should keep it covered depends on the type of dressing.

Traditional plastic wrap

If your artist used ordinary plastic wrap, it is generally removed after a relatively short period according to the artist’s instructions.

Once removed, gently wash the tattoo.

Adhesive healing film

Products such as transparent adhesive tattoo films can sometimes remain on the tattoo for several days.

Do not automatically remove this type of dressing after a few hours. Follow the specific instructions from your tattoo artist and the manufacturer of the film.

If the film becomes excessively loose, leaks, or causes significant irritation, contact your tattoo artist for advice.

Washing Your Tattoo

Once you are instructed to remove the initial covering, wash your hands thoroughly before touching the tattoo.

Then:

  1. Gently rinse the tattoo with lukewarm water.
  2. Use a small amount of mild, fragrance-free soap.
  3. Clean the area gently with your fingertips.
  4. Do not scrub or use a washcloth.
  5. Rinse thoroughly.
  6. Pat the tattoo dry with a clean paper towel or allow it to air dry.

Avoid rubbing the tattoo with a regular towel.

The skin may feel warm, tender, or slightly swollen during the first few days. Some redness and a small amount of clear or blood-tinged fluid can also be normal shortly after getting tattooed.

Moisturizing Your Tattoo

Once the tattoo is clean and dry, apply a very thin layer of a suitable fragrance-free moisturizer or the aftercare product recommended by your artist.

The goal is to moisturize the skin without covering it with a thick layer of product.

Too much moisturizer can leave the tattoo excessively wet and may contribute to irritation.

A good rule

Thin layer, clean skin, and don’t overdo it.

As the tattoo heals, you can continue moisturizing when the skin feels dry or tight.

What to Expect While Your Tattoo Heals

Tattoo healing happens in stages.

Days 1–3

The tattoo may be:

  • Red
  • Tender
  • Slightly swollen
  • Warm
  • Producing a small amount of clear fluid or plasma

The tattoo can also look significantly darker or shinier than it will once healed.

Days 4–14

The tattoo may begin:

  • Peeling
  • Flaking
  • Itching
  • Feeling dry
  • Looking slightly dull

Do not scratch or peel the tattoo.

Allow the skin to naturally shed.

Weeks 2–4+

The surface of the skin may appear healed while deeper layers continue recovering.

The tattoo can temporarily look slightly cloudy or less vibrant during this period.

Everyone heals at a different rate, and larger or heavily worked tattoos can take longer.

Don’t Scratch or Pick

One of the hardest parts of tattoo aftercare is dealing with the itching.

As the tattoo heals, peeling and itching are normal.

However, scratching or picking at the skin can:

  • Remove healing skin prematurely.
  • Irritate the tattoo.
  • Cause additional wounds.
  • Increase the risk of infection.
  • Potentially affect the appearance of the finished tattoo.

If the tattoo is itchy, gently pat or tap the surrounding area instead of scratching it.

Keep Your Tattoo Away From the Sun

Fresh tattoos should be protected from direct sunlight.

UV exposure can irritate healing skin and contribute to fading of tattoo pigments over time.

During the initial healing period, the simplest approach is to keep the tattoo covered with clean clothing and stay out of direct sunlight.

Once the tattoo is fully healed, use a broad-spectrum sunscreen when exposing it to the sun.

Regular sun protection can help preserve the appearance of your tattoo for years.

Avoid Swimming While It Heals

Avoid submerging a fresh tattoo in:

  • Swimming pools
  • Hot tubs
  • Lakes
  • Rivers
  • The sea
  • Baths

The problem is not simply getting the tattoo wet. Prolonged soaking can interfere with healing, while swimming environments can expose an open wound to microorganisms and chemicals.

A normal shower is generally fine once your artist has instructed you to remove the initial covering.

Shower: yes. Soaking: no.

Wait until the tattoo has fully healed before returning to swimming or prolonged soaking.

Be Careful With Clothing

Friction can irritate a healing tattoo.

Try to wear clean, comfortable clothing that does not constantly rub against the tattoo.

This is particularly important for tattoos on:

  • Ribs
  • Waist
  • Thighs
  • Feet
  • Ankles
  • Shoulders
  • Areas underneath tight clothing

If clothing sticks to the tattoo, do not pull it away aggressively. Moisten the material with lukewarm water and gently release it.

Exercise and Sweating

You do not necessarily need to stop exercising completely, but you should consider the tattoo’s location and how much friction, stretching, sweat, and contact it will experience.

Be particularly careful with:

  • Heavy sweating
  • Tight gym clothing
  • Equipment rubbing against the tattoo
  • Contact sports
  • Shared equipment touching the fresh tattoo

Avoid activities that could contaminate, damage, or repeatedly irritate the healing tattoo.

Things You Should NOT Put on a Fresh Tattoo

Avoid applying random household products or heavily fragranced cosmetics to a healing tattoo.

Unless recommended by your tattoo artist or healthcare professional, avoid:

  • Perfumed lotions
  • Strong exfoliants
  • Scrubs
  • Alcohol-based products
  • Hydrogen peroxide
  • Harsh antiseptics
  • Thick layers of petroleum-based products
  • Makeup over the healing tattoo

More products do not necessarily mean better healing.

Simple aftercare is usually better.

Don’t Re-Bandage It Yourself

Once the initial dressing has been removed, don’t automatically wrap the tattoo in plastic again.

A fresh tattoo needs appropriate protection, but continuously sealing it under improvised plastic can trap moisture and irritate the skin.

If your tattoo artist has specifically instructed you to use another dressing, follow their instructions.

Signs That Your Tattoo Is Healing Normally

Some symptoms are expected during normal healing.

These can include:

  • Mild redness around the tattoo
  • Tenderness
  • Mild swelling
  • Itching
  • Peeling
  • Flaking
  • A small amount of clear fluid shortly after tattooing

These symptoms should generally improve rather than progressively worsen.

Warning Signs

A tattoo is an injury to the skin, so infection is possible.

Contact a healthcare professional if you develop concerning symptoms such as:

  • Increasing rather than improving redness
  • Significant or worsening swelling
  • Severe or increasing pain
  • Pus or foul-smelling discharge
  • Fever or chills
  • Red streaks extending away from the tattoo
  • Skin that becomes increasingly hot and painful
  • Symptoms that continue to get worse rather than improve

If you are concerned that your tattoo may be infected, don’t try to treat a serious infection yourself. Seek medical advice.

What About Allergic Reactions?

Some people can experience reactions to tattoo pigments or products used during the tattooing process.

Seek medical attention if you develop significant symptoms such as:

  • Severe swelling
  • Extensive rash
  • Blistering
  • Difficulty breathing
  • Swelling of the face, lips, or throat

Difficulty breathing or swelling of the face, lips, or throat can indicate a serious allergic reaction and requires urgent medical attention.

A Simple Tattoo Aftercare Routine

For most people, tattoo aftercare can be kept relatively simple:

Morning

  1. Wash your hands.
  2. Gently clean the tattoo if needed.
  3. Pat it dry.
  4. Apply a thin layer of fragrance-free moisturizer.

During the day

  • Keep it clean.
  • Avoid unnecessary touching.
  • Don’t scratch or pick.
  • Avoid excessive friction.
  • Keep it away from direct sunlight.
  • Don’t submerge it in water.

Evening

Repeat the gentle cleaning and moisturizing routine if necessary.

Continue this general approach while the tattoo is healing and follow your artist’s specific instructions.

Long-Term Tattoo Care

Tattoo aftercare doesn’t completely stop once the peeling is finished.

The biggest long-term enemy of tattoo appearance is UV exposure.

Once your tattoo is fully healed:

  • Use sunscreen when it will be exposed to sunlight.
  • Keep your skin moisturized.
  • Avoid unnecessary tanning.
  • Stay hydrated.
  • Take care of your skin generally.

A well-cared-for tattoo can remain vibrant for many years.

Tattoo Aftercare Checklist

  • Follow your tattoo artist’s dressing instructions.
  • Wash your hands before touching the tattoo.
  • Clean the tattoo gently.
  • Pat it dry rather than rubbing it.
  • Use a thin layer of suitable moisturizer.
  • Don’t scratch or pick peeling skin.
  • Avoid swimming and soaking until healed.
  • Protect the tattoo from direct sunlight.
  • Avoid unnecessary friction.
  • Don’t apply harsh or fragranced products.
  • Watch for symptoms that are getting worse instead of better.
  • Contact a healthcare professional if you suspect an infection.

Final Thoughts

Good tattoo aftercare doesn’t need to be complicated.

Keep it clean, keep it appropriately moisturized, don’t pick at it, protect it from unnecessary irritation, and give it time to heal.

Your tattoo artist knows the exact products and techniques used on your skin, so their aftercare instructions should take priority over generic advice.

And remember: a tattoo may look finished when you leave the studio, but the skin underneath still needs time to recover.

Take care of the skin, and you’ll give the artwork the best chance of looking great for years to come.
